# Founding Maker Pilot

Simple Stipple is looking for 10 working laser makers to test a real job, report where the workflow breaks, and help shape the 1.0 release.

## Who this is for

Prioritize people who regularly prepare artwork for diode, CO2, fiber, or galvo laser work and who can safely inspect an exported DXF or SVG in their usual machine software. Good pilot users make custom gifts, jewelry, signs, leather goods, awards, or small-batch products.

## The promise

> Turn an image, drawing, or existing vector file into an editable engraving pattern without hand-building repetitive geometry.

The pilot is about the design-preparation workflow. Simple Stipple does not control laser hardware; every export must still be inspected and simulated in the user's machine software before a job runs.

## What each pilot tester receives

- A current desktop build and a short setup guide.
- A suggested test job matched to their material and machine workflow.
- Direct feedback review on one non-sensitive file or workflow description.
- Early access to fixes that result from the pilot.

Do not promise a specific future feature, support response time, or machine compatibility until it is confirmed.

## What to ask for

Ask every tester to complete one 20–30 minute workflow:

1. Import a simple logo, outline, or photo they are permitted to share.
2. Trace or clean it, then generate a Stipple Dots, Honeycomb, Voronoi, or Mesh pattern.
3. Export SVG or DXF.
4. Inspect it in their normal machine software; no machine run is required.
5. Report the first confusing step, missing option, export problem, and one capability they would pay for.

Record machine type, software used, input format, output format, whether the first export opened correctly, and the exact workflow they were trying to complete. Never request customer artwork, credentials, or material settings that they cannot share.

## Success criteria

The pilot is useful if at least 7 of 10 testers can produce an export that opens correctly in their normal machine software, and at least 3 describe a recurring workflow valuable enough to pay for. Use those recurring workflows to decide the paid 1.0 scope.

## Outreach message

```text
Hi [name],

I saw your [specific material / product / machine workflow]. I make Simple Stipple, a local desktop tool that turns an image, drawing, or DXF/SVG into editable engraving patterns such as stipple, honeycomb, and Voronoi.

I am recruiting 10 working laser makers to test it on one non-sensitive job. I am especially trying to learn whether it saves time when preparing [their relevant workflow].

Would you be open to a short pilot? You would import a sample, make a pattern, export it, and tell me where it breaks. I am not asking you to run unverified files on a machine.

Download and short demo: https://github.com/KiidxAtlas/simple-stipple/releases/latest
```

Personalize the second sentence. Send no more than one follow-up, five to seven days later, and stop if there is no response.

## Paid-product test

Do not introduce pricing in the first pilot message. After a tester completes a job, ask:

> If this reliably saved you from manually building patterns or repairing artwork, would a one-time $49–79 desktop license be reasonable? Which capability would make that an easy yes?

This tests willingness to pay without pretending the current pre-1.0 build is already a finished commercial product.
